# 1.1: vertical shorts and smooth jump cuts

The build plan's 1.1 row (`../plan.md` › Releases), decided here before building (2026-11-25, `video`). What the person
sees first, then how it's made.

## What the person does

1. In the export dialog, a new first row: **For: YouTube | Shorts**. *Shorts* means 9:16, for YouTube Shorts, TikTok and
   Reels (one preset; their specs agree: 1080 × 1920, 30 or 60 fps as the clip, AAC, under 3 minutes for Shorts; the
   dialog says when the edit runs longer).
2. The preview shows the 9:16 frame as soon as *Shorts* is picked, following the speaker, so what exports is what they
   saw. A **Shorts** switch beside *Captions* in the preview does the same before export.
3. Captions in a short use the **shorts styles**: big words in the lower third, two to three words a line, the word
   being said lit (the word-by-word style, larger and placed where phones don't cover it).
4. **Smooth jump cuts** (G17): a switch in the clean-up panel, off by default. At every silence cut where a face is in
   frame, the next stretch punches in to about 108% around the face, alternating in and out, so the cut reads as a
   new angle instead of a jump. Each zoom is a block on the timeline the person can drag shorter or delete.

## How it's made

| Part | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Face detector | **MediaPipe Face Detector** (BlazeFace short-range), `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` 1.2.x, Apache-2.0; the model (~230 KB) and its WASM ship in the app | Runs in the window on the frames we already decode, offline, on every system; no native add-on, no download wait. Short-range is for faces within about 2 m, which is a talking video. InsightFace's SCRFD is non-commercial (left out); YuNet would need ONNX Runtime in the window too |
| Where it runs | The window, CPU (WASM) delegate, on frames sampled 4 a second from the proxy or original, at 320 px wide | GPU delegates vary by driver (GitHub's machines have none); 4 a second at 320 px is a few ms a frame, so an hour's clip is tracked in about a minute, in the background after captions |
| Track | The largest face per sample, linked frame to frame by overlap; a gap under 2 s is held, longer goes to the frame's centre | One speaker is the case; the largest is the nearest |
| Camera path | The 9:16 window's centre follows the face's with a dead zone (no move while the face stays in the middle third) and a critically damped spring (no overshoot, about 0.6 s to settle), starting each kept stretch already on the speaker. The faces are what's stored, per clip (`faces.json`, 4 a second); the path is made again from them and the edit whenever it changes, a few milliseconds for an hour (built 2026-11-25: storing keyframes too would be a second copy that goes stale at every cut) | A camera that chases every head movement is the first complaint about auto-reframe (Opus Clip, CapCut users); a steady frame with slow pans reads as a person operating it |
| Compositor | The crop is a uniform in the existing WebGL2 compositor (`preview/compositor.ts`), used by the preview and the export alike | Preview equals export, as for captions today |
| Zoom blocks | A list on the edit (`edit/`), each `{ from, to, scale, centre }`, made at Clean up when the switch is on, undoable like any edit | The project file keeps them; removing one is one undo step |
| Window CSP | `script-src 'self' 'wasm-unsafe-eval'`; `connect-src 'self' media:` | WASM needs it; scripts still come only from the app. MediaPipe's web build posts usage numbers to Google every minute with no switch (found 2026-11-25): the CSP refuses them, and the main process refuses any web request from the window as a second lock |

## Proof before it ships (gate)

- On five test clips (one speaker centred, one walking left to right, two people, a face leaving the frame, a screen
  recording with no face), the face stays inside the 9:16 frame in at least 95% of sampled frames, and the frame
  doesn't move while the speaker sits still (`scripts/check-shorts.ts`).
- An exported short is 1080 × 1920, in sync (the flash and beep), with captions in the lower third.
- The demo's minutes measured: a 20-minute talk → captions, cleaned up, a 9:16 short.
