What it does

Captions

Your words appear as they're heard, in English and 24 other European languages. Show them word by word or as subtitles, in the video or as a file for YouTube.

Long pauses, cut

It marks every long pause on the transcript and the timeline. Cut them all in one click, keep the ones you want, and undo anything.

A clean voice

One switch takes the hum and the room noise out from under your voice. Before you turn it on, it says how long it will take.

How it works

  1. Drop in a video

    From your phone, camera or screen recorder. The transcript and captions start at once. The first time, it downloads its speech model, about 670 MB, once.

  2. Check the cuts

    Press Clean up and watch it play without the pauses. Click any pause or word to put it back, or split and delete parts yourself.

  3. Export

    Up to 4K, at the quality and loudness YouTube recommends, with the captions in the video or beside it. No watermark, ever.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes: every export, 4K included, with no watermark, no account and no ads. Nothing free in it today will ever become paid. It's open source, too: anyone can read and build its code.

Does my video leave my computer?

No. The captions, the cuts and the clean voice are all made on your computer, and it works without the internet once its speech model is downloaded. What does connect: the model downloads, update checks, and anything you choose to send us.

Which languages does it caption?

English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Russian, Ukrainian and 15 more European languages. More languages come in a later version.

Will it run on my computer?

On a Mac with macOS 13 or later (Apple chip or Intel), Windows 10 or 11, and 64-bit Linux. No graphics card needed. Downloads and what each needs.

How is it different from CapCut?

Every export is free, it needs no account, and your videos never go to anyone's servers. CapCut does far more today; %APP_NAME% does the clean-up of a talking video well and keeps adding.
